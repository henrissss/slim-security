Choosing the right camera system for your property

Wired (PoE) vs. wireless systems

Most of our installs use Power over Ethernet (PoE) IP cameras, which send video and power over a single cable back to a local recorder. PoE systems tend to hold up better over time — no batteries to replace, no Wi-Fi interference dropping frames at the worst moment, and cleaner 24/7 recording. Wireless cameras still have a place — rentals, additions, or spots where running cable isn't practical. We install both, and during your free assessment we'll tell you honestly which fits your property.

Residential vs. commercial installations

A single-family home usually needs 4–8 cameras covering entry points, the driveway, and the backyard, wired into your home network with remote viewing from your phone. Commercial surveillance jobs — retail storefronts, multi-family buildings, warehouses — typically call for more cameras, wider fields of view, longer footage retention for liability and insurance purposes, and sometimes integration with access control or intercom systems. We design and install commercial surveillance systems as well as home setups, and equipment scales to the property, not a one-size package.

NVR recording & storage

Footage is only useful if it's actually being recorded and easy to find later. Every install includes a Network Video Recorder (NVR) set up on-site that stores continuous or motion-triggered footage locally, so you're not stuck with a mandatory cloud subscription. Retention typically runs anywhere from a couple of weeks to a couple of months depending on drive size and camera count, and you get an app to pull up live or recorded footage from your phone.

Camera placement that actually works

Where a camera sits matters more than the camera itself. We aim for overlapping coverage at every entry point — front door, back door, garage — a driveway angle that actually catches license plates, and height/angle choices that avoid glare, direct sun, and easy tampering. A poorly placed 4K camera catches less useful footage than a well-placed 2MP one; that's a judgment call we make on-site, not something a generic package can guess at.
